# What to Say to This Company

## Use this skill when

The account is chosen and the message is not.

This is the point where most outbound quietly dies. The list is fine, the targeting is fine, and then someone opens a blank email, cannot find anything specific to say, reaches for the template, and sends the same paragraph that the other forty vendors sent that quarter.

Use it for one company at a time. It is not a list-processing tool, and running it across two hundred accounts to generate two hundred openers produces exactly the generic output it exists to avoid.

## Required input

- The company name and website.
- Who you sell to there, by role.
- What you actually do, in one honest sentence.

Anything else that is genuinely available helps and none of it is mandatory:

- their careers page, pricing page, or recent announcements
- the specific person's profile or posts
- whether anyone at your company has spoken to them before
- what happened with similar companies you have won or lost

## Analysis workflow

1. Establish what this company visibly does, in their own words, before forming any opinion about what they need.
2. Look for observable facts with commercial meaning: hiring in a function, a pricing model change, a market they entered, a product line they dropped, a job ad that describes the problem you solve.
3. Separate the observable from the assumed, and keep them separate in the output. "They posted three SDR roles" is observable. "They are struggling with pipeline" is a guess dressed as insight, and buyers can hear the difference immediately.
4. Form one hypothesis about a problem they plausibly have, tied to something specific you found, and state what would disprove it.
5. Write the opener around that one thing. One angle, not three.
6. Sanity check it with the substitution test: replace the company name with a competitor's name. If the message still works, it is a template and it goes back to step 2.
7. Give the honest fallback. Sometimes there is nothing specific to find, and the right move is a short, plain, low-claim message rather than a manufactured observation.

## Decision rules

- One angle per message. Two angles read as a pitch, three read as a brochure.
- Observable beats clever. A boring true fact about their business outperforms a smart guess about their strategy.
- Never invent a trigger event. Fabricating a funding round, a hire or a launch is the fastest way to lose an account permanently, and the buyer always knows.
- If the research turns up nothing usable, say so. "No specific angle available, send the short version" is a valid and useful answer.
- Congratulating someone on something public is not an angle. It is a subject line pretending to be one.

## Output format

### What is actually observable

Bulleted facts with where each came from. Nothing inferred in this section.

### The hypothesis

One paragraph: what you think is true about their situation, why, and what would prove you wrong.

### The opener

The message, ready to send, short.

### The substitution test

State whether the message survives having the company name swapped, and if it does, what was too generic.

### If the hypothesis is wrong

The one-line pivot for the reply that says "that is not really our situation."

## Practical example

A logistics software company is on the list. The site says nothing useful. But their careers page has two open roles for customer support in a company of forty people, and the job description mentions handling order status questions manually.

The observable fact is two support hires and a manual process named in a job ad. The hypothesis is that support volume is growing faster than headcount can absorb, and that order status is the dominant ticket type. The opener names the job ad, names the specific ticket type, and asks one question about how they handle it today.

The substitution test passes: swap in a competitor and the message breaks, because the competitor is not hiring for that.

## Guardrails

- Do not fabricate company facts, funding, headcount, customers or events.
- Do not present a guess as something you found.
- Do not scrape or store personal data beyond what is needed to write one message.
- Do not produce bulk personalisation. This skill makes one message for one company.
- Do not claim the message will get a reply. It removes a reason to ignore you, which is not the same thing.
